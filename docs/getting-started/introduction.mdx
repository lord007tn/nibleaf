Nibleaf is a tool for writing and publishing documentation, such as a help
center or product guide. You write pages in your browser, preview them, and
publish them as a website your readers can browse, search, and ask questions.
English and Arabic, including right-to-left layout, work out of the box.

## What you can do with Nibleaf

- **Write in the browser.** The editor works like a word processor. Behind the
  scenes, every page is saved as Markdown, a plain-text format you can take with
  you.
- **Publish safely.** Your changes stay in a draft until you select **Publish**.
  Readers never see a half-finished page.
- **Help readers find answers.** Published sites include search. You can also
  turn on **Ask AI**, a chat assistant that answers from your docs and links to
  its sources.
- **Learn what readers need.** Analytics show popular pages, searches that
  found nothing, and pages readers rated as unhelpful.
- **Work as a team.** Invite teammates, leave comments on a page, and connect an
  AI assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude to draft fixes for you.

## Choose Cloud or self-hosting

### Nibleaf Cloud

Choose [Nibleaf Cloud](/getting-started/cloud) to start writing right away. We
run the service; you manage your content and your team.

Nibleaf Cloud is free during its beta. It does not yet come with an uptime or
support guarantee, so keep your own export of important content.

### Self-hosting

Choose [self-hosting](/getting-started/self-hosted) when you need Nibleaf and its
data on servers you control. You get the source code and installation tools. You
also take on the operator work: servers, domain names, security certificates,
databases, email, backups, monitoring, and upgrades.

Before choosing either path for important documentation, read the
[known limitations](/reference/known-limitations).

## Learn the words Nibleaf uses

- **Site:** one documentation website. Each site has its own pages, settings, and
  team. The **Sites** list in the dashboard shows every site you belong to.
- **Members and roles:** the people who work on a site. An **Owner** or **Admin**
  manages settings and the team. An **Editor** writes and publishes. See
  [Invite your team](/product/team).
- **Draft:** the version of your pages you are editing. The editor saves it
  automatically and shows **Draft saved**.
- **Published version:** a frozen copy of your drafts that readers see. Each
  publish creates a new numbered version.

## Understand how publishing works

When you select **Publish**, Nibleaf copies your current drafts and site settings
into a new version and builds it in the background. Readers keep seeing the
previous version until the new one is ready, so a failed publish never takes
your site down. If a new version turns out to be wrong, you can restore the
previous one. See [Publish your changes](/product/publishing).

A published version is not a backup. It does not protect your drafts, team, or
settings. Export your content regularly; see
[Export and archive](/product/exports).

## Try a first success

Before you move a large knowledge base, publish a small two-page site:

1. Write an introduction that says who the docs are for.
2. Add a second page and link to it from the introduction.
3. Preview both pages, then publish once.
4. Open the site in a private browser window, where you are signed out.
5. Search for a phrase from the second page.

You have a good baseline when both pages open, the link works, and search finds
the second page. Continue with the [Cloud tutorial](/getting-started/cloud) or
the [self-hosted tutorial](/getting-started/self-hosted).
